# Cover Letter

**To:** IPFS Foundation Grants Committee
**From:** Ndibe Kefua, Founder — NDN Analytics
**Re:** Implementations & Integrations grant application — NDN IPFS Chain
**Date:** April 2026

---

Dear IPFS Foundation Grants Committee,

I'm writing to submit a $50,000 / 12-month grant proposal under the Implementations & Integrations program for **NDN IPFS Chain** — an open-source SDK, CLI, and public-gateway stack designed to make IPFS the default first choice for application developers instead of their fallback when S3 feels too centralized.

The full proposal, budget, milestones, and team document are attached. I want to use this letter for what letters are for: three short things that don't fit in a spec.

**First — this is not a pitch for vapor.** As of the submission date, the core infrastructure is already deployed and reachable. The Fastify API is live on Google Cloud Run with a Pinning Services–compatible surface, the Next.js dashboard is live alongside it, and the repository at `github.com/dnkefua/ndn-ipfs-chain` is public and Apache-2.0. I'd rather you click the URLs in §3 of the application than take my word on execution capacity.

**Second — this is a solo-developer application, and I want to be upfront about that.** I am the sole engineer on NDN IPFS Chain. I have one silent partner who holds equity but does no operational work. The commercial side of NDN Analytics (pharma provenance and healthcare-data pipelines, running since 2023) funds roughly 80% of this project's operating cost; the grant is requested specifically for the public-good pieces — open SDKs, free public gateway, Pinning Services API v1.0 conformance suite, developer tutorials — that the commercial customers do not pay for but that measurably lower the onramp friction for every IPFS developer, not just NDN's.

I've been deliberate about scope. A $150K ask with a 4-FTE team would have been easy to assemble on paper; it also would have been fiction. $50K funds what one developer plus commercial-business subsidy can realistically deliver in 12 months, with clean milestone gates and money returned to the Foundation if gates are missed. If Year 1 lands well, I'll earn the right to submit a larger Year-2 proposal on the strength of shipped metrics.

**Third — why this matters to IPFS specifically.** The competitive landscape (Pinata, Filebase, what's left of Web3.Storage) is good enough to keep professional web3 teams on IPFS, but it's not good enough to pull a junior backend engineer off S3 on a Tuesday afternoon. The difference is the first-five-minutes experience — SDKs that feel like any other cloud SDK, a free public gateway that's reliable enough to actually demo, and docs that show a dApp being built end-to-end, not just a CID being pinned. That's the gap this grant closes, and it's a gap whose closing benefits every IPFS provider, not just this one.

Thank you for reviewing. I'm glad to answer any questions or demo any part of the live stack on request.
